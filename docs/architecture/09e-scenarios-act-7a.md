### 9.8 Act 7: Every role, the agent's front door, and the bill

**What this section proves.** Act 6 followed one request through every layer. Act 7 answers the three questions a bank asks next (`console/catalog.py:743#story`), and 9.8.1 to 9.8.9 cover the first two. First, the agent enters through the same gateway as its tools: the user calls APIM, APIM checks the user's Entra token for the Foundry audience and forwards it unchanged to the Foundry project, and the agent's own tool calls come back through the same APIM (beat 7.1, 9.8.3). Second, the wall works for every kind of person and not only the one on the stage: Priya, Wes, Tara, Carla and Ian each make one allowed and one refused request with their own token, and the console shows which of walls 1 to 3 decided each (beat 7.2 and the five person tabs, 9.8.4 to 9.8.9). The administrator's agent runs, the cost board and the runaway-agent budget are the third question and are in 9.8.10 to 9.8.15. Every result is a real call, a real read of Azure role assignments or a read of the logs; where a person has not signed in, the console says "predicted, not run" instead of pretending (9.8.2; `console/act7.py:8-12#design`).

**Preconditions.**

1. **The platform and the front door are deployed.** Beats 7.1 and 7.4, and the "through the front door" button of the agent path, refuse to start with "run scripts\deploy.ps1 -Stage frontdoor first" when the front door is not deployed (`console/act7.py:142-159#deploy`).
2. **The operator is signed in.** Every operator token is minted silently from the Azure CLI cache and a missing account stops with "run `az login`" (`console/azure_probe.py:73-90#token`).
3. **Each person signed in once.** A person tab runs as that person only after the one-time device-code sign-in (Acting as, key P); until then the console raises `NeedsSignIn` and the matrix shows that person as predicted (`console/personas.py:193-212#token`, `console/catalog.py:744#order`).
4. **The kill switch is not engaged.** Job kinds that call the gateway or an agent (`e2e`, `matrix`, `e2e_agent`, `quota` and others) are refused with "The kill switch is still engaged. Click Reset demo (top right) first" while the kill switch is engaged; the read-only kinds `frontdoor`, `agentpath` and `finops` are not on that list (`console/jobs.py:495#NEEDS_OPEN_GATEWAY`, `console/jobs.py:511-518#killEngaged`; 9.1.5).
5. **Patience with the logs.** Each person-tab beat answers in seconds, an Admin agent beat in 15 to 90 seconds; Log Analytics shows a request 2 to 18 minutes later and a Sentinel incident 5 to 15 minutes later, and Azure Cost Management lags by hours (`console/catalog.py:745#wait`).
6. **Run 7.4 last and alone.** The Overview and 7.3 only read; 7.4 changes two APIM named values for about a minute and puts them back, so the catalog says to run it last and never while another beat is running (`console/catalog.py:744#order`). The job manager enforces most of that: the kind `quota` is a disruptive kind and conflicts with every gateway, agent and other disruptive job, while read-only kinds never conflict (`console/jobs.py:473-492#DISRUPT`).

#### 9.8.2 Role-matrix vocabulary and coverage

`Diagram: docs/diagrams/05-authorization-layers.excalidraw`

This subsection is the key to reading 9.8.3 to 9.8.14. It fixes the four evidence words of the Overview matrix, the tones and tags that classify every beat, what "designed, not run" means and how the console computes it, how three hand-written lists are kept consistent, and how one person-tab beat is run. The shared board of 9.1 keeps its own vocabulary (evidence tags in 9.1.3, verdicts in 9.1.6, outcome shapes in 9.1.7); the matrix, the coverage table and the person header add the words below. Section 7.6 holds the role model that these words refer to.

**The four evidence words of the matrix.** **Implemented.** The Overview matrix has six lanes, one per person and one for the administrator, and 13 cells: two per person and three for the administrator (`console/act7.py:223-262#LANES`, `console/web/render.js:202-204#administrator`). Every cell carries one of four evidence words, defined in the module docstring (`console/act7.py:8-12#design`) and drawn as a chip (`console/web/render.js:206-211#MX_EVIDENCE`):

| Word | Chip caption | What the cell is | Built by | Cells |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `live` | request just made | a real call through the gateway with that person's own token; the administrator lane uses the operator's token | `_gateway_cell` (`console/act7.py:320-334#_gateway_cell`) | 11 |
| `rbac` | Azure role assignment, read live | the grant itself, read from Azure role assignments at the resource group and at the workspace; not a request | `_rbac_cell` over `_infosec_grants` (`console/act7.py:342-361#_infosec_grants`) | 1: Ian's first cell |

| `design` | predicted by the role model - not run | the outcome the role model predicts, with no request behind it | `_design_cell` (`console/act7.py:303-310#_design_cell`) | 0 to 10, depending on who has signed in (the administrator's three cells are never predicted) |

Four things matter when reading a screen. (1) Four words, three chip styles: `rbac` is drawn with the `live` style (`console/web/render.js:208#rbac`), and `design` keeps the base dashed muted chip because colour rules exist only for `live`, `logs`, `replay`, `wait` and `portal` (`console/web/style.css:272-273#tr-ev`); an unknown word falls back to the `design` style and shows the raw word (`console/web/render.js:216#MX_EVIDENCE`). (2) A `design` cell reads "EXPECTED ALLOWED" or "EXPECTED REFUSED", has a dashed border, no status, seconds or request id, and no match marker (`console/web/render.js:230#EXPECTED`, `console/web/style.css:415#is-design`, `console/web/render.js:217-219#match`); a `live` cell instead shows "as the role model predicts" or "NOT as the role model predicts", and only a `live` cell lists the roles found in the token (`console/web/render.js:225#evidence`). (3) The wall chip is drawn only on a refused cell (`console/web/render.js:232#wallChip`). (4) A lane shows one of three pills, "Ran live", "Not signed in" or "A check failed", and a lane that waits for a sign-in has a dashed border (`console/web/render.js:243-246#matrixLane`, `console/web/style.css:402#st-signin`); the card banner is red when any live cell did not behave as predicted, amber when a lane waits or no live cell ran, and green otherwise (`console/web/render.js:255-262#matrixCard`).

The matrix words are not the evidence tags of the Act 6 board (9.1.3). A person-tab beat runs on the Act 6 board, because every scenario of `role_tabs.py` is an ordinary Act 6 board scenario with the same twelve rows (`console/role_tabs.py:1-9#ordinary`); its rows use the 9.1.3 words, and a person who has not signed in cannot run it at all (see "What design is not" below).

The page uses tone and tag in two places: the pill of the beat (`console/web/pages.js:271#rt-pill`) and the wording of the walk button, which says "Next: the refused case", "the allowed case", "the audit case", "the before picture" or "the attack case" (`console/web/pages.js:155-156#kindOf`, `console/web/pages.js:298#WHAT`). The coverage table of the Overview has its own five states, Allowed, Refused, Both, Before and Designed, not run (`console/role_story.py:460-466#LEGEND`), drawn as "Allowed", "Refused", "Both", "Before" and, for a designed cell, "Would allow" or "Would refuse" (`console/web/pages.js:151#COVER`, `console/web/pages.js:313#Would`).

**What "design" means and how it is computed.** **Implemented.** "Designed, not run" and "predicted by the role model" mean one thing: the console shows the outcome that section 7.6 predicts for a person and makes no request. It arises in three places.

1. A matrix cell of a person who has not signed in. `_lane` asks the local sign-in cache whether the person has an account (`personas.signed_in`, an offline check: `console/personas.py:172-190#signed_in`). If not, the lane state becomes `signin`, its note says the person "has not signed in yet, so these are the outcomes the role model predicts, not requests", and every cell of the lane is built by `_design_cell` (`console/act7.py:386-389#signed_in`). If a signed-in person's token cannot be obtained silently in the middle of a lane, only that cell becomes a design cell and the lane turns to `signin` (`console/act7.py:398-400#NeedsSignIn`).
2. How a design cell is computed. `_design_cell` calls no service. It reads the lane's hand-written `want` (verdict, wall, reason) and `expect` text, the title and plain words of the reason from `catalog.REASONS`, and `_words` (`console/act7.py:303-310#_design_cell`); `_cell` leaves status, seconds and request id empty and `as_expected` at `None` (`console/act7.py:291-301#as_expected`). `LANES` is therefore a second hand-written copy of the role model of section 7.6, and a design cell is only as correct as that copy (see the limits below).
3. A coverage cell for the private Positions server. `DESIGNED` says what each person's cell predicts: Priya, Wes and Ian refused, Tara and Carla allowed (`console/role_story.py:530-536#DESIGNED`). `coverage()` turns each entry into a cell of state `design` that points at the Admin beat `PRIVATE_BEAT` (`console/role_story.py:576-578#DESIGNED`), and the reach row of a person tab draws the server chip with "designed, not run" (`console/web/pages.js:249#designed`). The reason is architectural: the private server is reachable only from the agent's subnet and is not behind APIM, so no person can call it directly (`console/role_story.py:528-529#_PRIVATE_NOTE`); the Admin tab runs it through the agent (9.8.10 to 9.8.12). `_roles` ties each entry to the person's Positions.Read role (`console/role_story.py:676-681#DESIGNED`, `console/personas.py:101-105#can_use`).

What design is not: a person-tab beat has no design mode. If the person has not signed in, the scenario does not start; `e2e.validate` answers "This scenario is pinned to <Name>: open Acting as ..., sign in as <Name> once, then run it again." (`console/e2e.py:1256-1260#pinned`). So "predicted, not run" appears only in the matrix, the coverage table and the reach row, and never as the result on a person board (`docs/DEMO-GUIDE.md:121#designed`).

**How coverage() and verify() keep the lists consistent.** **Implemented.** The role story is three hand-written lists that must agree: the beats, tabs and presenter text of `console/role_story.py`; the scenario registry `SCENARIOS` of `console/e2e.py`, into which `role_tabs` registers the persona and Admin scenarios (`console/e2e.py:1875#role_tabs`, `console/role_tabs.py:561-575#_register`); and the persona cases `CASES` of `console/role_tabs.py` (`console/role_tabs.py:90#CASES`). The beats are ordinary catalog beats plus `tab`, `tone` and `tag` (`console/role_story.py:7-8#tone`).

`coverage()` is computed, not written. It walks every beat and every alternate button, looks the scenario up in `SCENARIO_COVERS` (31 scenario ids, each naming the deployed pieces it exercises and whether it ends allowed, refused or before: `console/role_story.py:477-510#SCENARIO_COVERS`), groups the results by deployed piece and tab, and gives each cell one state: `both` when an allowed and a refused result both occur, else `refused`, else `before`, else `allowed` (`console/role_story.py:547-554#_state`). The rows are the 12 deployed pieces (`console/role_story.py:512-526#ARTIFACTS`) and the columns are the six tabs that have a person or the operator (`console/role_story.py:557-581#coverage`). #### 9.8.3 Beat 7.1: The agent's front door: user -> APIM -> agent

`Diagram: docs/diagrams/11-agent-front-door.excalidraw`

**Purpose.** Answer the first question of Act 7: does the agent enter through the same gateway as its tools? The beat shows one more API on the same APIM instance as the MCP gateways, `abw-agent-frontdoor` at the path `/agents`, which sits between the user and the Foundry project's Responses endpoint. A person who talks to an agent now passes a gate of the same kind as a person who calls a tool: a token check, the kill switch (the same named value that the MCP gateways use), a per-user call rate and a per-user token budget, all in one policy (`infra/08-agent-frontdoor.bicep:1-8#AFTER`, `infra/apim/policies/agent-frontdoor.policy.xml:4-9#independently`, `infra/apim/policies/agent-frontdoor.policy.xml:6#MCP`). The user's own token goes through unchanged, so Foundry still decides what the person may do. The test button runs four probes and makes no model call: no token, a token for the wrong resource, a token for Foundry and, for honesty, the same request sent to Foundry without APIM. Three alternates run a real agent call that enters at the front door and switch the console's agent calls between the front door and the direct path (the before picture). Narration note: the beat opens with "Until now the user called the agent directly" (`console/catalog.py:753#directly`) and its runner says "Act 6 sent the user straight to the agent" (`console/act7.py:175#straight`), but `agent_via()` answers `apim` as soon as the front door is deployed (`console/jobs.py:105-107#agent_via`), so in a deployed environment the agent beats of Acts 1 to 6 already enter at the front door (step 1 of 9.2.3; Findings F7a-1). Provenance: Implemented (runner, policy, path switch), Configured (API, operation, named values), Platform behaviour (the three APIM policies; the Microsoft Learn pages are named under "Why and trade-offs").

**Actors and tokens.** The caller is the user of the agent; in the demo it is the operator. The token is the user's front-door token: an Entra access token whose audience is the Foundry data plane, `https://ai.azure.com`. The probe calls take it from the Azure CLI's sign-in cache with `ap.token(AI_AUDIENCE)` and send it as a bearer credential (`console/act7.py:200-208#AI_AUDIENCE`, `console/azure_probe.py:73-89#token`); the agent child process builds its credential from the same cache (`src/agent/ask.py:109-112#build_credential`). The front door never replaces the token: the policy sets no Authorization header and uses no managed identity, and its header comment says that Foundry still authorises the person and that the gateway "never swaps the caller for a service identity" (`infra/apim/policies/agent-frontdoor.policy.xml:10-12#UNCHANGED`). The wrong-audience probe uses a genuine Entra token for Azure Resource Manager, taken from the same sign-in (`console/act7.py:191-198#ARM`). The tool calls that the agent makes while it runs are not requests to the front door: Foundry's runtime sends them to the gateway URLs of the MCP servers with the per-user tool token that it holds, minted at consent or at the first agent call after the previous one expired (`src/agent/abw_agents.py:95-107#server_urls`, `src/agent/abw_agents.py:119-139#project_connection_id`, `DESIGN.md:223#Foundry`; steps 4 to 9 of 9.2.3). The console also reads the API definition from Azure Resource Manager with the operator's ARM token (`console/act7.py:176-178#serviceUrl`).

**Preconditions.** The front door is deployed: the stage `frontdoor` of `scripts\deploy.ps1` runs after the `apim` stage, deploys `infra/08-agent-frontdoor.bicep` with the Foundry project endpoint built from the resource names, and writes the state file that the console reads (`scripts/deploy.ps1:19#frontdoor`, `scripts/deploy.ps1:392-398#Invoke-Stage`, `console/jobs.py:98-102#frontdoor_ready`). Without it the test button answers "The APIM agent front door is not deployed in this environment - run scripts\deploy.ps1 -Stage frontdoor first." and starts nothing (`console/act7.py:142-151#validate`). The operator is signed in with `az login`, because the probe tokens and the agent's credential come from that cache (`console/azure_probe.py:73-89#token`). Probe 4 of the test button (the honest check, step 6 below) needs Foundry's public network access to be on, which it is in this demo (`infra/06-governance.bicep:284-293#abw-ex-foundry-public-endpoint`; Findings F7a-2). For the agent alternate, the preconditions of 9.2.1 hold (baseline applied, the operator holds the Falcon role and has consented once), the kill switch is not engaged, because the `agent` kind needs an open gateway (`console/jobs.py:495#NEEDS_OPEN_GATEWAY`, `console/jobs.py:511-518#killEngaged`), and the front door has its default limits, which beat 7.4 changes and Reset demo restores (9.8.14, `console/act7.py:132-139#restore_frontdoor`).

**What the front door does, in order.** **Configured.** The policy is loaded as raw XML into the API's policy resource. The API has three operations, one each for GET, POST and DELETE, all with the URL template `/*`, and `subscriptionRequired` is false, so Entra is the only credential (`infra/08-agent-frontdoor.bicep:49-87#subscriptionRequired`, `infra/08-agent-frontdoor.bicep:64-74#methods`). Its two settings are APIM named values, `abw-agent-token-quota` (default 250,000 tokens per hour) and `abw-agent-rpm` (default 60 calls per minute); both are demo defaults and not a sizing (`infra/08-agent-frontdoor.bicep:17-47#tokenQuotaPerHour`, `DESIGN.md:56#abw-agent-frontdoor`). The inbound section runs these stages in order:

| # | Stage | What it does | Refusal | Where |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | `validate-jwt` | requires a signed, unexpired bearer token (60 s clock skew) whose audience is `https://ai.azure.com`, whose issuer is this tenant's v1 or v2 endpoint and whose `tid` claim is this tenant; it checks no client (`azp`), scope or role | 401 | `infra/apim/policies/agent-frontdoor.policy.xml:18-32#validate-jwt` |
| 2 | variables | reads `oid` and the user name from the validated token, the agent name from `agent_reference.name` in the request body (else `n/a`), and sets the deny flag to `revoked` when `oid` is in `abw-deny-oids` | none | `infra/apim/policies/agent-frontdoor.policy.xml:33-55#abw-deny` |
| 3 | decision telemetry | writes the metric `abw-gateway-decisions` and the trace `abw-gateway` with server `agents`, decision `allow` or `deny` and reason `ok` or `revoked`, before the limits run | none | `infra/apim/policies/agent-frontdoor.policy.xml:56-70#abw-gateway` |
| 4 | kill switch | when the deny flag is set, answers 403 with code `forbidden` and reason `revoked` and stops, so the agent and the model are never reached | 403 | `infra/apim/policies/agent-frontdoor.policy.xml:71-94#return-response` |
| 5 | `rate-limit-by-key` | allows `abw-agent-rpm` calls per 60 seconds, counted per `oid` | 429 | `infra/apim/policies/agent-frontdoor.policy.xml:95#rate-limit-by-key` |
| 6 | `llm-token-limit` | allows `abw-agent-token-quota` tokens per hour, counted per `oid` after the response and not estimated (`estimate-prompt-tokens` is false), and sets the headers `x-abw-tokens-remaining` and `x-abw-tokens-consumed` | 403 | `infra/apim/policies/agent-frontdoor.policy.xml:96#llm-token-limit` |
| 7 | `llm-emit-token-metric` | records tokens by `oid`, user name and agent in the namespace `abw-agent` (the cost view of 9.8.13) | none | `infra/apim/policies/agent-frontdoor.policy.xml:97-101#llm-emit-token-metric` |
| 8 | request id and forward | sets `x-abw-request-id` to the APIM request id on the forwarded request and forwards it to Foundry, waiting up to 240 seconds for the response headers | none | `infra/apim/policies/agent-frontdoor.policy.xml:102-108#forward-request` |
| 9 | outbound headers | sets `x-abw-request-id` and `x-abw-gateway` with the value `apim-agent-front-door` on a forwarded response; the refusals set the same two headers themselves (lines 78-83, 127-132 and 168-173) | none | `infra/apim/policies/agent-frontdoor.policy.xml:109-117#apim-agent-front-door` |

Two properties of this order matter for the rest of the beat. The gate is thinner than the MCP gateway's: there is no client allow-list and no role or scope check, because the front door authenticates and meters the person and leaves authorisation to Foundry (Findings F7a-3). And the first trace record of stage 3 is written before the two limits run, so a request that the limits refuse leaves two trace records, an `allow` and a later `deny` (see "Evidence produced").

The three questions that a bank is likely to ask have answers in the beat. "Foundry checks Entra tokens too, so why put APIM in front?" The identity check is the same; what Foundry does not give is a place of the bank's own for a per-user call rate and token budget, a kill switch that works on a live token, one log for every user-to-agent call and token metering by person (`console/catalog.py:768-769#Foundry`). "Does the gateway swap the user for a service identity?" "Does the legacy agent use the front door?" No, on purpose: it is the before picture (`console/catalog.py:776-777#legacy`). The talk track opens the beat with "Nobody calls the agent directly. Say: "One gateway for every hop. The agent is not a side door around the controls the bank already knows how to audit: the same token check, the same kill switch, the same log." (`console/catalog.py:764#sales`)

**Why and trade-offs.**

- **The token passes through; the gateway does not become the caller.** **Implemented.** The front door authenticates and meters the person and leaves authorisation to Foundry and the tools, which still see the user (`infra/apim/policies/agent-frontdoor.policy.xml:10-12#UNCHANGED`, `console/catalog.py:772-773#swap`). The price is that the gate is thinner than the MCP gateway's: no allow-list of client applications and no role check (Findings F7a-3).
- **Both limits are counted per object id.** One runaway person is not everybody's outage, and the cost line and the quota line name the same person (`infra/apim/policies/agent-frontdoor.policy.xml:95-96#llm-token-limit`, `observability/kql/AbwTokenUsage.kql:3#identity`). There is one default budget for everybody; a per-department budget is a design option and is not built (`docs/DEMO-GUIDE.md:249#per-department`).
- **The token budget is counted after the response.** **Platform behaviour.** The policy sets `estimate-prompt-tokens` to false, so it counts the usage that Foundry reports; the call that crosses the line still completes and the next call is refused, and concurrent calls can briefly exceed the limit. The hourly period starts at the UTC hour, the policy answers 403 when the quota is exceeded and 429 only for a tokens-per-minute rate (which the policy does not set), and the remaining-tokens header that the console shows is an estimate (`infra/apim/policies/agent-frontdoor.policy.xml:96#estimate-prompt-tokens`, `docs/DEMO-GUIDE.md:249#overshoot`, `README.md:149#overshoot`, `console/catalog.py:87-92#allowance`; https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/api-management/llm-token-limit-policy; Findings F7a-6).
- **The call rate is a sliding window and never completely accurate.** **Platform behaviour.** `rate-limit-by-key` answers 429 with a `Retry-After` header once a user exceeds `abw-agent-rpm` calls in the window of 60 seconds, and a window is at most 300 seconds; the demo notes a lock-out of about two minutes after a burst (`infra/apim/policies/agent-frontdoor.policy.xml:95#rate-limit-by-key`, `docs/DEMO-GUIDE.md:249#lock-out`; https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/api-management/rate-limit-by-key-policy).
- **A backend error passes through, and the wait is capped at 240 seconds.** **Platform behaviour.** `forward-request` does not turn a backend 4xx or 5xx into an error unless `fail-on-error-status-code` is set, which the policy does not do, so Foundry's own errors reach the caller with the two APIM headers; the timeout is the wait for the response headers and values above 240 seconds may not be honoured (`infra/apim/policies/agent-frontdoor.policy.xml:106-108#forward-request`; https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/api-management/forward-request-policy). The console's child process waits up to 420 seconds in all (`console/jobs.py:717#timeout`).
- **The front door is a gateway, not a wall around the model.** Foundry's own endpoint still answers a valid token because public network access is on, under a policy exemption whose text names the production step (public network access off and a private endpoint) (`infra/06-governance.bicep:284-293#abw-ex-foundry-public-endpoint`; Findings F7a-2). The private MCP server sits outside APIM, so the kill switch and the call rate do not reach it (`README.md:149#outside`; Findings F7a-10). The legacy agent bypasses the front door on purpose.
- **The limits are demo defaults.** 250,000 tokens per user per hour is about fifty demo agent runs, chosen so that it never trips by accident, and 60 calls per minute is not a sizing either (`infra/08-agent-frontdoor.bicep:17-18#fifty`, `DESIGN.md:246#sizing`).
- **The before/after switch is a console flag, not a control.** It changes which base URL the console's agent client uses; it does not close the direct path in Azure. The script `agent_run.py` itself defaults to `direct` when it is run by hand without `--via` or `ABW_AGENT_VIA`, while the console always passes `--via` (`console/agent_run.py:77-78#ABW_AGENT_VIA`, `console/jobs.py:721#argv`; Findings F7a-7).
- **The policy cannot be shown in the console.** The console's policy viewer reads only the MCP servers, so this policy is a portal step that the beat's own text calls "not yet seen in this portal" (`console/catalog.py:763#viewer`; Findings F7a-9).
- **Rows count requests, not model rounds.** The playbook says that an agent run is "several model rounds, one row each", but the child counts one Responses call per approval round, and APIM writes one row per request (`docs/PROOF-PLAYBOOK.md:1003#rounds`, `console/agent_run.py:95#rounds`; Findings F7a-8).

The validation has no rule for `matrix`, and `persona_error` ignores every kind that is not a persona kind, so Acting as is not consulted (`console/jobs.py:503-533#start`, `console/jobs.py:588-591#act7`, `console/jobs.py:595-599#PERSONA_KINDS`, `console/act7.py:142-157#validate`).
2. The wrapper imports `act7` on first use and calls `act7.run_matrix` (`console/jobs.py:1371-1374#run_matrix`, `console/jobs.py:1419#matrix`).
3. `run_matrix` says why the beat exists and adds the step "Running six lanes in parallel: Priya, Wes, Tara, Carla, Ian and the administrator". It starts a pool with one worker per lane, so all six lanes begin together (`console/act7.py:411-417#ThreadPoolExecutor`).
4. Each worker runs `_lane` (B). When a lane finishes, the job adds one step: "<Name>: not signed in - shown as designed" (state info) or "<Name>: N of M behaved as the role model predicts" (state ok, or fail when a live cell surprised), and then checks for a Stop (`console/act7.py:419-429#as_completed`).
5. When all six are done, the lanes are put back in the fixed order (Priya, Wes, Tara, Carla, Ian, Administrator), three notes are added (walls 1 to 3 only, the evidence legend, the private server that is not on the board) and one `matrix` event carries lanes and notes (`console/act7.py:434-444#notes`).
6. The job ends with a summary and a status: `failed` if any live cell was not as predicted, else `done` (`console/act7.py:445-448#unexpected`, `console/jobs.py:296-304#finish`).
7. Console -> browser: the page draws the event with `matrixCard` (`console/web/render.js:596-606#matrixCard`).

*B. One lane (`act7._lane`).*

1. Header: for a person, the name, the job title from the setup record, the one-line story and the role chips; for the administrator, the fixed name "Administrator" and role chips decoded from the operator's tokens (`console/act7.py:372-385#STORY`).
2. Gate: if the person has no saved sign-in, the lane gets the state `signin` and a note that says that these are predicted outcomes, not requests, and asks for the sign-in through Acting as; every cell is the predicted one and no request is made (`console/act7.py:386-389#signin`).
3. Otherwise the cells run one after the other: `special: rbac` is the role-assignment read, `special: audit` the log query, and every other cell a gateway request (`console/act7.py:390-397#special`).
4. A `NeedsSignIn` raised while a cell runs (the saved sign-in has expired or was revoked) sets the lane to `signin`, keeps the exception text as the note and turns that cell into the predicted one; any other exception becomes a cell with the verdict `failed` and the title "Could not run this check", and sets the lane state `error`; a Stop is re-raised (`console/act7.py:398-406#NeedsSignIn`, `console/personas.py:208-210#expired`).

*C. One live gateway cell (`act7._gateway_cell`).*

1. G1: `jobs.call_gateway` gets the token for the cell's server and scope (the person's own, or the operator's for the administrator) and sends one JSON-RPC `tools/call` POST to the server's gateway URL with a fresh `x-abw-request-id` (`console/jobs.py:452-462#call_gateway`, `demo/mcpclient.py:124-144#x-abw-request-id`).
2. G2 to G9 run as in 9.1.8; the decision falls at the hop that the table names.
3. `http_result` reads the status, the decision, the reason and the request id (from the structured content, the body or the response header), and `judge` turns them into a verdict: denied for HTTP 401, 403 or 429 or a decision of DENIED or BLOCKED, allowed for a 2xx answer, failed otherwise (`console/jobs.py:229-252#http_result`, `console/jobs.py:176-198#judge`).
4. `_wall_of` names the wall from the shape of the answer, `_words` picks the title and the plain words, and `_as_expected` compares verdict, wall and reason with what the lane expects (`console/act7.py:270-277#_wall_of`, `console/act7.py:280-288#_words`, `console/act7.py:312-317#_as_expected`).
5. The cell keeps the status, the seconds, the request id, the roles of the token and the first 500 characters of the answer (`console/act7.py:320-333#_gateway_cell`).

**Why and trade-offs.**

- **Implemented. One click, six lanes, no agent and no model.** The answers are deterministic and arrive in about ten seconds. The price is that wall 0 is not exercised by this beat; the Foundry guardrail belongs to Beat 4 and Act 6 (`console/act7.py:440#Walls`).
- **Implemented. A person who has not signed in is predicted, not tested.** `_design_cell` builds the cell from the same reason texts that a live cell uses, and the dashed card, the "EXPECTED" badge, the lane note and the amber banner keep a prediction from passing as a result (`console/act7.py:303-309#_design_cell`, `console/catalog.py:46-108#REASONS`, `console/web/render.js:230-233#EXPECTED`). The weakness is that the presenter has to say it aloud, and three of the six lanes are predictions until Wes, Tara and Carla have signed in once (Findings F7a-12).
- **Implemented. The wall is inferred from the shape of the answer.** A 401, 403 or 429 is wall 1, a 200 that carries DENIED or BLOCKED is wall 2, and only the two reason codes in `DATA_PLANE_REASONS` move it to wall 3, so a new data-plane reason code that is not listed there would be drawn as wall 2 (`console/act7.py:266-277#DATA_PLANE_REASONS`).
- **Implemented.
